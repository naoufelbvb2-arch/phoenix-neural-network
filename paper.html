Methods note · spiking neural networks · falsification

What survives
the controls

Six “self-organisation” claims in a temporal spiking network. Five looked positive. Every one dissolved under a matched control — and the small thing left standing is designed, not emergent.

The thesis. In this temporal spiking network (“Phoenix”), the things that work are designed and distributional — not learned, not emergent. Six independent emergence claims each began as a number well above chance, or a structure that appeared to arise from the network’s own dynamics. Each was real as a number and false as an explanation. The matched control — a condition identical in every respect except the one the claim credits — is what separated the two. The controls are the method; the surviving core is the result; and the fact that five of the six looked positive before controlling is the argument for the method.

01

A matched control is not optional — it is the experiment

A recurring failure mode made this essential: the natural “destroy-the-structure” control — a shuffle — is too weak. It was the stricter controls that did the work: rate-matching, a random-input training arm, network partitioning, direct a-priori generation. Where a shuffle would have certified a positive, the stricter control dissolved it. Twice this happened to results the author was hoping to keep — including the constructive positive below.

02

Six claims, six dissolutions

  1. 1
    Recurrent computation dissolved promised: state-space separation. Dissolved by a rate- and structure-matched feed-forward control — it was delay lines, not recurrence.
  2. 2
    STDP learns a useful gain dissolved promised: 92% “pinned at w_max”. The random-input arm scored identically on noise; the 92% was the weight clamp doing its job.
  3. 3
    Synaptic scaling shapes weights dissolved promised: a useful weight spread. The spread is better drawn a priori (log-normal) than produced by the controller.
  4. 4
    State space encodes order dissolved promised: 100% order separation. Delete all recurrent synapses → separation unchanged. It was reading delay lines.
  5. 5
    Capacity scales with network size dissolved promised: super-linear Kmax growth. Pin the readout / partition into blocks → a connected 64k net equals four disconnected 16k blocks.
  6. 6
    Delay plasticity learns cue structure dissolved promised: 43%→68% from a local rule. The random-input arm matched it; the gain is a delay distribution, not learning.

Two are worth stating in full, because the strict control reversed a result a shuffle would have passed.

Capacity — the partition test

From 16k→64k, capacity (Kmax, patterns held at ≥50%) grew super-linearly — apparently a strong case for scale. But three things grew at once: the network, the readout (25% of N), and the cue (4% of N). Hold the readout at the 16k size and Kmax returns to the 16k value; and a connected 64k network scores identically to four disconnected 16k blocks — 85.7% vs 85.9% at K=1280 — with matched cells, synapses, cue and readout. Global connectivity contributes nothing. The large network is four small ones in a trenchcoat.

Delay plasticity — the random-input arm

A local, post-spike-free delay rule took a memory task from 43% to 68%. It beats the shuffle control, so a shuffle-only design would have called it the project’s first survivor. Train the same rule on random input and evaluate on the cues: it scores the same (67%). The structure is real but not cue-specific. Confirmed across two orthogonal rules, five seeds each.

03

What survives

Controlling the claims away leaves a coherent, positive picture — small, designed, temporal.

The surviving core

  • A feedforward delay-line + coincidence detector — what every dissolved claim turned out to be really doing.
  • Log-normal weights — a shape fact, matched on mean throughout, better drawn than learned.
  • Delay mean — a magnitude fact: arrival timing matched to the readout lag, reported at a fixed lag/span ratio.

On the delay lever specifically, two measurements must be read together. Versus a one-hop line, recurrence wins (40.4% vs chance). Versus multi-hop chains of matched depth, it loses (a designed chain equals or beats it at every depth, 100% vs 62% at three hops). Reconciled: recurrence is an inefficient generator of multi-hop depth — the same thing a chain does deterministically, only noisier and at a stability cost. The lever is delay-line depth, best obtained by design.

04

The controls, as a reusable toolkit

  • Rate-matching. Match firing rate between conditions — to the post-manipulation rate. Rate differences masquerade as structure.
  • Random-input arm. Train the rule on random input, evaluate on the task. Separates learning from a generic parameter drift. Dissolved claims 2 and 6.
  • Partition. Compare a structure against the same units with the structure removed. Dissolved claim 5’s integration reading.
  • Direct generation. If a rule “learns” a configuration, draw it directly and see if the gain survives. Turned claims 3 and 6 into distributional facts.
  • Watch the readout. Readout size and stimulus size both scale with N and both inflate results — the confound behind claim 5, and behind a retracted result below.
05

The stability apparatus is unnecessary

If recurrence is an inefficient way to get depth that design supplies directly, everything the recurrence cost becomes optional. Recurrence produced, all measured here: bistability with a zero-width operating band; metastable ignition after ~3000 ticks; homeostasis failing three ways; synaptic scaling that stabilised but damaged function; inhibition required only to stop runaway; per-spike delivery-order complexity.

A feedforward delay line has none of these — no cycles, so no runaway, so no ignition, so no homeostatic controller, no inhibition-for-stability, provable termination, trivial parallelism, and a firing rate that is a direct function of the input rather than an equilibrium to be regulated. The negative on recurrence is a large positive on architecture.

06

The constructive test

Everything above is negative. The constructive form: build the architecture the analysis implies — an explicit feedforward delay-line + coincidence detector, no cycles, no inhibition, no homeostasis, no plasticity, log-normal weights, matched mean delay — and run it against the recurrent architecture on every benchmark.

It matches. On capacity, read fairly (each architecture from its own most-active cells), the two are equal (~98–100% at K=1280). On memory depth it equals or beats the recurrent net. And it is far more robust to readout: with a random readout it holds 97% while the recurrent net drops to 35%, because feedforward concentrates the response while recurrence disperses and mixes it.

An honest correction, kept in the record because it is the point: a first pass showed feedforward “beating” recurrent 6× on capacity. That was a readout-selection artifact — the same confound as claim 5. Give the recurrent net its own best cells and the gap closes. The claim is match, not beat. The method, applied to the paper’s own positive.

08

Scope, and what this does not show

One codebase, one implementation of each rule, synthetic tasks, no real data, a single rate-coded readout, one weight/delay regime; means over 3–5 seeds at N ≤ 64,000. The method generalises; the results are about this architecture on these tasks.

These dissolutions do not show that emergence fails in spiking networks, that these mechanisms fail in general, or that recurrence is useless. They show that these formulations, in this architecture, on these tasks, produced nothing their matched controls did not — and that a designed feedforward core reproduces the same behaviour more simply here. STDP, synaptic scaling, recurrent computation, and delay plasticity may each carry real structure under other encodings, tasks, or rules. This is evidence about one system, not a verdict on the ideas.

Every promising number stayed a hypothesis about a confound until a matched control ruled it out — including the paper’s own constructive positive, twice. That is the contribution.
